## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how NovaTech Retail evaluates replenishment requirements and determines whether planned supply should be increased, maintained, reduced, or delayed.

The objective is to maintain sufficient inventory to support expected customer demand while limiting unnecessary excess inventory.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all active products managed through NovaTech Retail's standard replenishment process.

For the current planning model, inventory and demand from NovaTech's online and physical retail channels are managed as a single combined planning position for each SKU.

Replenishment decisions are made at SKU level.

## 3. Replenishment Assessment

Replenishment decisions must be based on the expected future inventory position rather than current inventory alone.

The planner should consider, where relevant:

* available inventory;
* approved demand forecast;
* recent actual sales;
* current Weeks of Supply;
* confirmed incoming supply;
* open purchase orders;
* expected supply arrival dates;
* supplier lead time; and
* applicable supplier ordering constraints.

Weeks of Supply thresholds must be interpreted according to POL-101.

Demand and forecast signals must be interpreted according to POL-102.

## 4. Projected Inventory Position and Planning Point

Before changing replenishment, planners should assess whether current inventory and confirmed incoming supply are expected to support demand over the relevant planning horizon.

For basic planning purposes:

**Projected Available Supply = Current Available Inventory + Confirmed Incoming Supply**

Expected demand during the relevant period must then be considered against the projected available supply.

A low current inventory position does not necessarily indicate a need for additional replenishment if sufficient confirmed supply is already expected to arrive before inventory is exhausted.

Similarly, a healthy current inventory position may still require additional replenishment if future demand is expected to consume inventory before replacement supply can arrive.

### 4.1 Replenishment Planning Point

When a replenishment requirement is being calculated and projected stockout exposure exists, the default replenishment planning point is the **first projected stockout week**.

The projected inventory position, forward demand rate, WOS, target inventory, gap-to-target, and replenishment requirement must all be evaluated using the same selected planning point.

Values from different planning weeks must not be combined when determining a replenishment requirement.

If a planner explicitly requests a different planning week, scenario, or what-if analysis, the requested planning point should be used instead of the default first-stockout planning point.

For example, if the first projected stockout occurs at CW+1, CW+1 should normally be used as the replenishment planning point. If the planner explicitly asks for the expected position at CW+3, the calculation should instead use CW+3 and the corresponding forward demand and projected inventory values.

If no projected stockout exists, the relevant planning point should be selected according to the planning question being evaluated and the available future inventory position.

## 5. Increase Replenishment

An increase in replenishment should be considered when the projected inventory position is expected to fall below NovaTech's target coverage range and existing confirmed incoming supply is insufficient to restore coverage.

Factors supporting an increase may include:

* projected WOS remaining below the 4–6 week target range;
* sustained demand above the approved forecast;
* insufficient confirmed incoming supply;
* expected stockout before the next feasible replenishment arrival; or
* an increase in the approved demand forecast.

A recommendation to increase replenishment must also consider supplier lead time and applicable ordering constraints.

Being below the target WOS range alone does not automatically require an additional purchase order.

## 6. Maintain Replenishment

The existing replenishment plan should normally be maintained when projected inventory is expected to remain within the target coverage range and no significant change in demand or supply is identified.

Maintaining the plan may also be appropriate when current WOS is below target but confirmed incoming supply is expected to restore inventory coverage within an acceptable timeframe.

Planners should avoid placing additional orders solely to correct a temporary low inventory position when sufficient replacement supply is already confirmed.

## 7. Reduce Replenishment

A reduction in future replenishment should be considered when projected inventory is expected to materially exceed expected demand and create potential overstock.

Factors supporting a reduction may include:

* projected WOS exceeding 8 weeks;
* sustained actual demand below forecast;
* a downward revision to the approved forecast;
* excessive confirmed incoming supply; or
* multiple open purchase orders creating unnecessary future inventory.

Any proposed reduction must consider whether existing purchase orders can still be modified under the applicable supplier terms.

## 8. Delay Replenishment

A delay should be considered when additional supply is still expected to be required but the currently planned arrival would create an unnecessarily high inventory position.

For example, a purchase order may be suitable for delay when:

* projected inventory exceeds the target coverage range before the PO arrival;
* the product is expected to return to the target range at a later date;
* the supplier permits the delivery date to be changed; and
* delaying the order does not create a future stockout risk.

Delaying a purchase order is different from cancelling or reducing it. The quantity remains required, but the planned arrival is moved to a later date.

## 9. Decision Priority

When evaluating replenishment, planners should prioritize avoiding stockout risk while also limiting unnecessary excess inventory.

The standard decision sequence is:

1. Determine the current inventory position.
2. Review the approved demand forecast and relevant recent sales.
3. Identify confirmed incoming supply and expected arrival dates.
4. Project inventory across the relevant planning horizon.
5. Identify potential stockout or overstock risk.
6. If stockout exposure exists and no alternative planning point was explicitly requested, select the first projected stockout week as the replenishment planning point.
7. Calculate the replenishment requirement consistently at the selected planning point.
8. Determine whether replenishment should be increased, maintained, reduced, or delayed.
9. Validate the proposed quantity against supplier ordering constraints.
10. Assess whether confirmed or standard replenishment can arrive in time.
11. Where timing risk exists, evaluate the appropriate action under POL-106.

## 10. Replenishment Recommendations

A replenishment recommendation must clearly state the proposed primary action and the main evidence supporting it.

The standard recommendation categories are:

* **Increase** — additional supply should be considered.
* **Maintain** — the current replenishment plan remains appropriate.
* **Reduce** — less future supply is expected to be required.
* **Delay** — the quantity may still be required, but at a later date.

When an Increase recommendation is supported by the planning assessment, the recommendation should state the valid replenishment quantity after applicable MOQ and order-multiple constraints have been considered.

The recommendation should present one primary replenishment quantity associated with the selected replenishment planning point. Alternative quantities from different planning points should not be presented as competing recommendations unless the planner explicitly requested a scenario comparison.

When projected stockout exposure exists, the recommendation should clearly identify the first projected stockout week and the associated shortage signal.

When confirmed or standard replenishment cannot arrive before the projected stockout, the recommendation should clearly identify the timing risk. Where supported by POL-106, the recommendation should also identify the appropriate timing action, such as reviewing an expedite or earlier-supply option.

The primary replenishment action and timing action should be distinguished clearly.

For example:

**Primary action:** Increase replenishment by the validated order quantity.

**Timing action:** Review expedite or earlier supply because standard replenishment cannot arrive before the projected stockout.

Recommendations should explain the relevant inventory, demand, incoming-supply, supplier-constraint, and timing conditions rather than relying only on the final category.


## 11. Exceptions and Planner Review

Replenishment policies provide a standard planning framework but cannot account for every business situation.

Products affected by significant promotions, launches, discontinuations, unusual supplier constraints, or exceptional demand events may require additional planner review.

Where available information is insufficient to determine the appropriate replenishment action, the planner should not make an unsupported recommendation.

## 12. Planning Principle

Replenishment decisions should be forward-looking.

NovaTech does not increase supply simply because inventory is currently low, nor reduce supply simply because inventory is currently high.

The appropriate decision depends on the expected inventory position after considering future demand, confirmed incoming supply, arrival timing, supplier lead time, and ordering constraints.

## 13. Policy Summary

NovaTech uses four standard replenishment recommendation categories: **Increase, Maintain, Reduce, and Delay**.

An increase should be considered when projected coverage remains below target and existing incoming supply is insufficient.

The current plan should normally be maintained when projected coverage remains healthy or confirmed incoming supply is sufficient to restore coverage.

A reduction should be considered when projected inventory creates material overstock risk.

A delay should be considered when supply remains necessary but its current arrival timing would create unnecessary excess inventory.

No replenishment action should be based on current WOS alone.
