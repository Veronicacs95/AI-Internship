# NovaTech Retail — Lead Time, Stockout Risk & Expedite Policy

Author: Supply Planning Operations

Effective date: 2026-01-01
Last review: 2026-06-01

Owner: Supply Planning
Approver: Director of Operations
Related: POL-101, POL-103, POL-104, POL-105


## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how NovaTech Retail uses supplier lead time, projected
inventory, projected stockout timing, and incoming-supply arrival dates to
identify supply risk and determine when expedited supply should be considered.

The objective is to identify situations where inventory may fall below the
required planning position or be exhausted before replacement supply can become
available, while avoiding unnecessary expedite actions and associated costs.


## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all active products managed through NovaTech Retail's
standard replenishment process.

For the current planning model, inventory and demand from NovaTech's online and
physical retail channels are evaluated as a single combined planning position
for each SKU.


## 3. Supplier Lead Time

Supplier lead time represents the expected time between placing a valid purchase
order and the ordered supply becoming available to NovaTech.

Lead time is measured in weeks for standard supply-planning purposes.

Lead times may differ by SKU and supplier and must therefore be obtained from
NovaTech's current approved product and supplier data.

This policy defines how lead time should be interpreted; it does not define a
single standard lead time for every product.


## 4. Lead Time and Replenishment Planning

Supplier lead time must be considered when evaluating the future inventory
position of a SKU.

Lead time must not be compared with current WOS alone. Planning decisions must
consider the projected inventory position at the earliest feasible replenishment
arrival date.

The assessment should consider:

* current available inventory;
* approved forecast demand expected during the lead-time period;
* confirmed incoming supply and its expected arrival dates; and
* the inventory expected to remain when new replenishment becomes available.

For example:

* Available inventory: 600 units
* Approved weekly forecast: 150 units
* Current WOS: 4 weeks
* Supplier lead time: 6 weeks
* No confirmed incoming supply

Although the SKU currently has 4 WOS and is within the healthy inventory range
defined in POL-101, the 600 units are expected to be consumed in approximately
four weeks.

A new standard order placed today would not become available for approximately
six weeks.

The SKU therefore has an estimated two-week stockout exposure before new
standard replenishment could become available.

Current WOS must therefore not be interpreted independently of supplier lead
time and projected inventory consumption.


## 5. Projected Stockout

A projected stockout occurs when available and confirmed incoming inventory is
expected to be exhausted before sufficient replacement supply becomes available.

For a basic assessment with constant weekly demand:

**Estimated Weeks to Stockout = Available Inventory / Approved Weekly Demand**

Where confirmed incoming supply exists, its quantity and arrival timing must
also be considered.

For more detailed planning, inventory should be projected by week using the
approved weekly forecast rather than assuming constant demand.

A projected stockout is a forward-looking risk indicator and does not mean that
the product is already out of stock.


## 6. Stockout and Below-Target Risk Before New Standard Supply

A SKU is considered exposed to stockout risk when projected inventory is
expected to reach zero before the earliest feasible arrival of sufficient
replacement supply.

A SKU may also have replenishment risk without reaching zero inventory.
Projected inventory at the replenishment arrival date should therefore also be
assessed against the inventory targets defined in POL-101.

For replenishment planning, POL-101 defines:

* Healthy inventory range: 4–6 WOS
* Standard planning target: 5 WOS
* Critical inventory: below 2 WOS
* Potential overstock: above 8 WOS

For example:

* Available inventory: 300 units
* Approved weekly forecast: 100 units
* Current WOS: 3 weeks
* Supplier lead time: 2 weeks
* No confirmed incoming supply

After two weeks of forecast demand, approximately 100 units would remain when
newly ordered supply could first become available.

The projected pre-arrival inventory position is therefore approximately 1 WOS.

Although the SKU does not stock out before the earliest feasible arrival, its
projected position is materially below the 5-WOS planning target.

The SKU therefore requires replenishment action even though an expedite may not
necessarily be required.

Stockout risk and below-target inventory risk must be evaluated separately.


## 7. Stockout Risk with Confirmed Incoming Supply

Confirmed incoming supply must be evaluated according to both its quantity and
expected arrival date.

For example:

* Available inventory: 200 units
* Approved weekly forecast: 100 units
* Current coverage: 2 weeks
* Confirmed incoming supply: 600 units
* Expected arrival: 5 weeks

Current inventory is expected to be exhausted in approximately two weeks, while
the confirmed supply is expected in five weeks.

This creates an estimated three-week stockout exposure despite the existence of
a confirmed purchase order.

The existence of an open purchase order alone must therefore not be interpreted
as evidence that the supply position is protected.

The treatment of confirmed incoming supply is defined in POL-104.


## 8. Expedite Definition

An expedite is an action intended to bring confirmed or required supply into
NovaTech earlier than the standard or currently expected arrival date.

An expedite may involve:

* requesting an earlier supplier ship date;
* prioritizing production or order processing;
* using a faster approved transportation method; or
* accelerating an existing purchase order where the supplier supports the
  change.

Expedited supply may result in additional cost and should therefore be used only
when justified by a material supply risk.


## 9. When Expedite Should Be Considered

An expedite should be considered when a projected stockout is expected to occur
before the next confirmed or feasible standard supply arrival and the resulting
inventory gap represents a material business risk.

Factors supporting an expedite may include:

* projected inventory reaching zero before confirmed supply arrives;
* confirmed incoming supply being delayed beyond the expected stockout date;
* sustained demand above forecast materially accelerating inventory consumption;
* an approved forecast increase creating an earlier stockout; or
* standard replenishment lead time being insufficient to prevent the projected
  stockout.

Critical inventory status under POL-101 should increase the priority of the
review but does not automatically require an expedite.

A below-target projected WOS position alone does not automatically justify an
expedite if standard replenishment can arrive without creating a material
stockout exposure.


## 10. When Expedite Is Not Required

An expedite should not normally be recommended when confirmed incoming supply is
expected to arrive before available inventory is exhausted and the arrival
timing does not create a material business risk.

For example:

* Available inventory: 300 units
* Approved weekly forecast: 100 units
* Current coverage: 3 weeks
* Confirmed incoming supply: 500 units
* Expected arrival: 2 weeks

During the two weeks before the confirmed supply arrives, approximately 200
units of forecast demand are expected to be consumed.

Immediately before receipt, approximately 100 units of existing inventory are
therefore expected to remain.

Following receipt of the 500 incoming units, projected inventory would be
approximately 600 units.

At the stated weekly demand rate:

**Projected WOS after receipt = 600 / 100 = 6 WOS**

The incoming supply therefore prevents the projected stockout and restores the
SKU to the healthy inventory range defined in POL-101.

Based on these conditions alone, an expedite would not be required.

However, arrival before stockout does not by itself prove that the future supply
position is adequate. The projected inventory position after receipt must also
be evaluated against the applicable inventory target.


## 11. Expedite Quantity

Where an expedite is justified, the expedited quantity should be sufficient to
address the expected supply gap without unnecessarily creating excess inventory.

The entire outstanding purchase-order quantity does not automatically need to be
expedited.

For example, where 1,000 units are scheduled to arrive but only 200 units are
required to bridge the projected inventory gap, planners should evaluate whether
a partial expedite is operationally possible.

Expedite quantities should address the identified timing gap while considering
the projected inventory position after receipt.

Any quantity adjustment must comply with applicable supplier constraints defined
in POL-105.


## 12. Delayed Purchase Orders

A delay to confirmed incoming supply must trigger reassessment when the revised
arrival date materially changes the projected inventory position.

For example:

**Original position**

* Estimated stockout: Week 5
* Confirmed PO arrival: Week 4

No inventory gap is expected.

**After supplier delay**

* Estimated stockout: Week 5
* Revised PO arrival: Week 7

The revised position creates an estimated two-week inventory gap.

An expedite or alternative replenishment action should therefore be considered.

The revised projected inventory position after receipt should also be reassessed
against the inventory targets defined in POL-101.


## 13. Demand Changes and Stockout Risk

Stockout timing and projected inventory must be reassessed when expected demand
changes materially.

Higher-than-forecast demand may cause inventory to be exhausted earlier than
previously expected and may reduce projected WOS at future supply-arrival dates.

Lower-than-forecast demand may extend available coverage and reduce or eliminate
a previously identified stockout risk.

Where weekly demand varies, the approved weekly forecast should be used to
project inventory across the relevant planning horizon rather than relying only
on a simple average.

Demand and forecast changes should be interpreted according to POL-102.


## 14. Expedite Recommendation

An expedite recommendation should identify:

* the affected SKU;
* the current inventory position;
* the projected stockout timing;
* the next expected or feasible supply arrival;
* the projected inventory position immediately before that arrival;
* the estimated inventory gap;
* the quantity potentially required to cover the gap; and
* the reason standard replenishment is insufficient.

Where appropriate, the projected inventory position following receipt should
also be compared with the planning target defined in POL-101.

Where the available information is insufficient to determine stockout timing,
incoming-supply timing, or projected inventory, an expedite should not be
recommended without further review.


## 15. Planning Principle

Current WOS, projected WOS, and stockout risk represent different planning
measures and must not be treated as interchangeable.

Current WOS describes the inventory position today.

Projected inventory and projected WOS describe the expected inventory position
at a future planning point after forecast demand and confirmed incoming supply
have been considered.

Stockout risk identifies whether inventory is expected to reach zero before
sufficient replacement supply becomes available.

Supplier lead time determines how far forward the planner must evaluate the
inventory position when considering new replenishment.

A product may therefore:

* have low current WOS but avoid stockout because confirmed supply arrives before
  inventory is exhausted;
* avoid stockout but remain materially below the planning target after supply
  arrives; or
* have healthy current WOS but face a future stockout because supplier lead time
  exceeds the remaining inventory coverage.

For this reason, lead time must be evaluated against projected inventory at the
earliest feasible replenishment arrival, not against current WOS alone.

Replenishment requirements should be evaluated against the standard planning
target defined in POL-101, while expedite decisions should additionally consider
whether the timing of standard or confirmed supply creates a material inventory
gap.


## 16. Policy Summary

Supplier lead time represents the expected time required for new replenishment
to become available to NovaTech and may differ by SKU and supplier.

Lead time must be evaluated using projected inventory rather than current WOS
alone.

Planning assessments should consider current inventory, approved forecast demand
during the lead-time period, confirmed incoming supply, and its arrival timing.

A projected stockout occurs when inventory is expected to be exhausted before
sufficient replacement supply becomes available.

A SKU may require replenishment action even when no stockout is projected if its
future inventory position remains below the planning target defined in POL-101.

An expedite should be considered when standard or confirmed supply cannot arrive
in time to prevent a material inventory gap.

Critical or below-target WOS does not automatically require an expedite.

The core planning assessment therefore considers both:

1. whether sufficient supply will arrive before inventory is exhausted; and
2. what the projected inventory position will be when that supply becomes
   available.

These conditions must be evaluated using the approved planning data and
inventory targets applicable to the SKU.
