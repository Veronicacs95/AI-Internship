
# NovaTech Retail — Purchase Orders & Incoming Supply Policy

Author: Supply Planning Operations
Document ID: POL-104
Effective date: 2026-01-01
Last review: 2026-06-01
Next review: 2027-01-01
Classification: Internal
Owner: Supply Planning
Approver: Director of Operations
Related: POL-101, POL-103, POL-105, POL-106

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how NovaTech Retail uses open purchase orders, confirmed incoming supply, and expected arrival dates when assessing future inventory availability.

The objective is to ensure that replenishment decisions consider supply already scheduled to arrive and are not based solely on current on-hand inventory.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all active products managed through NovaTech Retail's standard replenishment process.

For the current planning model, inventory and incoming supply are evaluated as a single combined inventory position for each SKU across NovaTech's online and physical retail channels.

## 3. Open Purchase Orders

An open purchase order (PO) represents an order placed with a supplier that has not yet been fully received by NovaTech.

An open PO should include, at minimum:

* PO number;
* SKU;
* ordered quantity;
* outstanding quantity;
* supplier;
* order date;
* expected arrival date; and
* current PO status.

Only the outstanding quantity that has not already been received should be treated as potential future supply.

## 4. Confirmed Incoming Supply

Open purchase orders should not automatically be treated as confirmed incoming supply.

For standard planning purposes, incoming supply is considered **confirmed** when:

* the purchase order has been accepted or acknowledged by the supplier;
* an outstanding quantity remains;
* a valid expected arrival date is available; and
* the order has not been cancelled or placed on hold.

Confirmed incoming supply may be included when evaluating the projected inventory position.

Unconfirmed, cancelled, or on-hold quantities must not be treated as confirmed future inventory.

## 5. Expected Arrival Date

The expected arrival date determines when incoming supply is expected to become available to NovaTech.

Incoming supply should only be considered available from its expected arrival date onward.

For example, if a product has 300 units currently available and 600 units confirmed to arrive in three weeks, the 600 incoming units must not be treated as part of today's available inventory.

The timing of the arrival must be compared with expected demand during the period before the supply arrives.

## 6. Current and Projected Inventory

Current inventory and projected inventory represent different planning views.

**Current Available Inventory** represents inventory available now.

For basic forward-looking planning:

**Projected Supply = Current Available Inventory + Confirmed Incoming Supply**

However, projected supply must also be evaluated against the demand expected before and after each supply arrival.

A simple addition of all future purchase orders to current inventory may produce a misleading result if the arrival dates are not considered.

## 7. Demand Before Supply Arrival

Planners must consider whether existing inventory is sufficient to support expected demand until confirmed incoming supply arrives.

For example:

* Current available inventory: 300 units
* Approved weekly forecast: 100 units
* Current WOS: 3 weeks
* Confirmed incoming supply: 600 units
* Expected arrival: 2 weeks

Although current inventory is below NovaTech's 4–6 WOS target range, existing inventory is expected to cover demand until the confirmed supply arrives.

In this situation, current WOS alone would not justify an additional purchase order.

## 8. Supply Arriving After Expected Stockout

A confirmed purchase order does not eliminate supply risk when it is expected to arrive after available inventory is exhausted.

For example:

* Current available inventory: 200 units
* Approved weekly forecast: 100 units
* Current WOS: 2 weeks
* Confirmed incoming supply: 600 units
* Expected arrival: 5 weeks

Current inventory is expected to be exhausted approximately three weeks before the incoming supply arrives.

This represents a potential stockout exposure and should be reviewed according to POL-106.

## 9. Multiple Open Purchase Orders

When multiple open purchase orders exist for the same SKU, each order should be evaluated according to its outstanding quantity and expected arrival date.

For example:

* PO-1001: 300 units arriving in 2 weeks
* PO-1002: 500 units arriving in 5 weeks
* PO-1003: 500 units arriving in 8 weeks

The planner should not simply treat all 1,300 units as immediately available future inventory.

Expected demand between each arrival should be considered when assessing the projected stock position.

## 10. Delayed Incoming Supply

When the expected arrival date of an open purchase order moves later, the projected inventory position must be reassessed.

A delay may create stockout risk even when the total incoming quantity remains unchanged.

Where a delayed arrival creates an expected period without sufficient inventory, the planner should review the appropriate replenishment or expedite action in accordance with POL-103 and POL-106.

## 11. Cancelled and On-Hold Purchase Orders

Cancelled purchase orders must be excluded from projected supply.

Purchase orders placed on hold must not be treated as confirmed incoming supply until the hold is removed and a valid expected arrival date is confirmed.

If a previously confirmed PO becomes cancelled or placed on hold, the affected SKU should be reassessed because the removal of expected supply may materially change its projected inventory position.

## 12. Partially Received Purchase Orders

When only part of a purchase order has been received, the received quantity becomes part of current inventory once it is available for sale.

Only the remaining outstanding quantity should continue to be treated as incoming supply.

For example:

* Original PO quantity: 1,000 units
* Quantity received: 600 units
* Outstanding quantity: 400 units

The remaining incoming supply associated with the PO is **400 units**, not 1,000 units.

## 13. Purchase Orders and Replenishment Decisions

Before recommending additional replenishment, planners must review existing confirmed incoming supply.

A product with low current WOS may not require additional replenishment when sufficient supply is already scheduled to arrive before a stockout occurs.

Conversely, a product may require action even when a large quantity is already on order if that supply is expected to arrive too late.

Replenishment decisions should therefore consider both:

**Quantity — how much supply is coming?**

and

**Timing — when will the supply arrive?**

Replenishment recommendation categories are defined in POL-103.

## 14. Planning Principle

Incoming supply must be treated as **time-dependent future inventory**, not as inventory available today.

The existence of an open purchase order alone is not sufficient evidence that inventory risk has been resolved.

NovaTech's projected inventory assessment must consider the outstanding quantity, PO status, expected arrival date, and demand expected before the supply becomes available.

## 15. Policy Summary

Open purchase orders represent potential future supply, while only valid and acknowledged orders with an expected arrival date should be treated as confirmed incoming supply.

Confirmed supply must be evaluated according to **both quantity and arrival timing**.

Incoming quantities must not be added to current inventory without considering the demand expected before their arrival.

Cancelled and on-hold purchase orders must be excluded from confirmed incoming supply, and only the outstanding quantity of a partially received PO should remain as future supply.

A low current WOS does not automatically justify additional replenishment when sufficient confirmed supply is expected to arrive before stockout.
