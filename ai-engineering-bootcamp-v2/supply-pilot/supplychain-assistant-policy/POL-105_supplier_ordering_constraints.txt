
# NovaTech Retail — Supplier Ordering Constraints Policy

Author: Procurement & Supply Planning
Document ID: POL-105
Effective date: 2026-01-01
Last review: 2026-06-01
Next review: 2027-01-01
Classification: Internal
Owner: Procurement
Approver: Director of Operations
Related: POL-103, POL-104, POL-106

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how NovaTech Retail applies supplier ordering constraints when creating or modifying purchase orders.

The objective is to ensure that replenishment recommendations are operationally valid and comply with supplier-specific requirements such as Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ), order multiples, and purchase-order restrictions.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all products purchased from external suppliers through NovaTech Retail's standard replenishment process.

Supplier constraints may differ by SKU and supplier. Planners must therefore use the current approved ordering parameters associated with the relevant SKU and supplier.

## 3. Minimum Order Quantity

Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ) is the smallest quantity that a supplier will accept for an order of a particular SKU.

A replenishment requirement below the applicable MOQ must not be submitted as a standard purchase order unless an approved exception has been agreed with the supplier.

For example, if:

* calculated replenishment requirement: 320 units;
* MOQ: 500 units;

the standard order cannot be placed for 320 units.

The planner should evaluate whether ordering the 500-unit MOQ would create an acceptable projected inventory position before placing the order.

## 4. Order Multiples

Some suppliers require quantities above the MOQ to be ordered in defined increments known as order multiples.

For example:

* MOQ: 500 units
* Order multiple: 100 units

Valid standard order quantities include:

* 500 units;
* 600 units;
* 700 units;
* 800 units.

Quantities such as 550 or 725 units would not satisfy the standard ordering constraint.

Where a calculated replenishment requirement does not match a valid order multiple, the proposed quantity should normally be rounded up to the next valid quantity, provided doing so does not create unacceptable overstock.

## 5. SKU-Specific Ordering Constraints

MOQ and order multiples are product and supplier attributes and must not be assumed to be identical across NovaTech's product catalogue.

For example, NovaTech may have the following approved ordering parameters:

* laptops: relatively low MOQ due to high unit value;
* accessories: higher MOQ due to lower unit value and case-pack requirements;
* monitors: medium MOQ depending on supplier shipping configuration.

The actual approved MOQ and order multiple for each SKU must be retrieved from NovaTech's current product and supplier data.

This policy defines how those constraints should be interpreted; it does not define the specific MOQ for every product.

## 6. Supplier-Specific Restrictions

Suppliers may apply additional ordering restrictions beyond MOQ and order multiples.

These may include:

* fixed ordering days;
* minimum total purchase-order value;
* case-pack quantities;
* maximum quantities per order;
* limited order modification windows; or
* restrictions associated with product availability.

Where a supplier-specific restriction exists in NovaTech's approved supplier data, it must be considered before an order recommendation is finalized.

## 7. New Purchase Orders

Before a new purchase order is created, the planner should determine the replenishment requirement based on the expected future inventory position.

The proposed quantity must then be validated against:

* the SKU's MOQ;
* applicable order multiple;
* supplier lead time;
* existing open purchase orders; and
* expected inventory coverage after the proposed order.

A valid supplier quantity does not automatically mean that the quantity should be ordered.

The resulting inventory position must remain reasonable according to NovaTech's inventory and replenishment policies.

## 8. Existing Purchase-Order Modifications

Supplier constraints may also apply when an existing purchase order is increased, reduced, delayed, or cancelled.

Before recommending a modification, planners should verify whether the supplier permits the requested change.

A supplier may reject a modification when:

* the order has entered production;
* the order has already shipped;
* the modification falls outside the permitted change window; or
* the revised quantity violates MOQ or order-multiple requirements.

Purchase-order status and expected arrival rules are defined in POL-104.

## 9. Increasing an Existing Purchase Order

When additional supply is required, increasing an existing open PO may be considered before creating a separate new order.

Any increase must comply with the applicable supplier ordering constraints.

For example, if an existing PO contains 500 units and the planner identifies an additional requirement of 150 units, but the supplier requires changes in multiples of 100 units, a standard increase of 150 units would not be valid.

The planner should evaluate a valid quantity such as 200 units and assess its impact on projected inventory.

## 10. Reducing an Existing Purchase Order

A reduction should only be recommended when the supplier permits the change and the resulting quantity remains valid under the applicable ordering constraints.

For example, reducing a 700-unit order to 450 units would not be valid where the MOQ is 500 units.

If the supplier does not permit a reduction, the planner should not assume that excess future supply can be removed from the projected inventory position.

## 11. Ordering Constraint Exceptions

Exceptions to supplier ordering constraints may occur when specifically agreed with the supplier or approved by NovaTech Procurement.

An exception must not be assumed simply because the standard MOQ or order multiple creates an inconvenient planning result.

Where no approved exception exists, the standard supplier constraint must be applied.

## 12. Relationship to Replenishment Recommendations

A replenishment requirement and an executable purchase-order quantity are not always the same.

For example:

**Calculated requirement = 420 units**

may become:

**Executable order quantity = 500 units**

because of supplier MOQ and order-multiple constraints.

SupplyPilot recommendations should distinguish between the underlying replenishment requirement and the final valid order quantity whenever supplier constraints affect the result.

The final quantity should also be checked to ensure that it does not create excessive projected inventory.

## 13. Planning Principle

Supplier ordering constraints define what NovaTech **can order**, while inventory and replenishment policies determine what NovaTech **should order**.

These concepts must not be treated as interchangeable.

A supplier may permit an order of 1,000 units, but that does not mean NovaTech requires 1,000 units.

Similarly, NovaTech may calculate a requirement of 320 units, but supplier constraints may make a 320-unit purchase order impossible.

The final recommendation must satisfy both the planning requirement and the applicable supplier constraints.

## 14. Policy Summary

NovaTech must comply with supplier-specific ordering constraints when creating or modifying purchase orders.

The **Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ)** defines the minimum acceptable order quantity, while **order multiples** define the valid quantity increments above the minimum.

MOQ and order multiples may differ by SKU and supplier and must be obtained from current approved product and supplier data.

A calculated replenishment requirement must therefore be validated against supplier constraints before it becomes an executable purchase-order recommendation.

Supplier constraints determine what can be ordered; they do not independently determine what should be ordered.
