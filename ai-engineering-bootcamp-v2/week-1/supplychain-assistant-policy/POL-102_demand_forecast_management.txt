## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how NovaTech Retail uses demand forecasts and historical sales to support inventory and supply-planning decisions.

The objective is to provide a consistent forward-looking demand signal for calculating inventory coverage, identifying changes in demand, and supporting replenishment planning.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all active products managed through NovaTech Retail's standard forecasting and replenishment process.

For the current planning model, demand from NovaTech's online and physical retail channels is combined into a single weekly demand forecast for each SKU.

Forecasts are maintained at SKU and week level.

## 3. Primary Demand Signal

The **approved weekly demand forecast** is NovaTech's primary forward-looking demand signal for supply planning.

The approved forecast represents the expected quantity of a product that NovaTech anticipates selling during each future week.

Supply-planning calculations that require expected future demand should use the approved forecast unless an approved forecast adjustment has been made.

Historical sales must not automatically replace the approved forecast.

## 4. Weekly Forecast

Each active SKU should have an approved forecast expressed as expected units per week.

For example, if the approved forecast for a product is 150 units per week, the expected demand over four weeks is:

**4-week expected demand = 150 × 4 = 600 units**

Where weekly forecasts differ across the planning horizon, planners should use the forecast associated with each individual week rather than assuming demand is constant.

## 5. Historical Sales

Historical sales represent actual units sold during completed periods.

Historical sales should be used to evaluate whether actual customer demand is developing differently from the approved forecast.

Recent sales performance is a supporting planning signal and should not automatically override the approved forecast.

A single unusually high or low sales week should not normally result in an immediate forecast change without additional evidence.

## 6. Forecast Variance

Forecast variance should be reviewed when recent actual sales materially differ from the approved forecast.

For standard planning review, a difference of more than **20% between average actual weekly sales over the most recent four completed weeks and the corresponding average weekly forecast** is considered a material variance.

For example:

* Average weekly forecast: 100 units
* Average actual weekly sales: 125 units
* Variance: +25%

This represents a material positive variance and should trigger forecast review.

A material variance does not automatically change the approved forecast. The planner should determine whether the difference represents a sustained change in demand or a temporary event.

## 7. Demand Increases

When recent actual sales are consistently above forecast, planners should investigate the reason for the increase.

Potential causes may include:

* sustained growth in customer demand;
* promotional activity;
* seasonal demand;
* product launches;
* competitor activity; or
* temporary sales events.

If the higher demand is expected to continue, the forecast should be reviewed and adjusted through the approved forecasting process.

Supply decisions should not rely solely on an isolated demand spike.

## 8. Demand Decreases

When recent actual sales are consistently below forecast, planners should review whether expected future demand remains realistic.

A sustained reduction in demand may increase future inventory coverage and create overstock risk if incoming supply continues according to the previous forecast.

Where lower demand is expected to continue, the forecast should be reviewed before additional replenishment is committed.

## 9. Forecast and Weeks of Supply

Weeks of Supply calculations should use the demand signal appropriate to the planning question.

For a simple current coverage assessment, the approved average weekly forecast may be used:

**WOS = Available Inventory / Approved Average Weekly Forecast**

For forward-looking supply analysis, the weekly forecast should be considered across the relevant future planning horizon.

WOS results should therefore be interpreted together with forecast changes and incoming supply rather than treated as a static measure.

Inventory coverage thresholds are defined in POL-101.

## 10. Forecast Review

Forecasts should be reviewed regularly for products showing significant differences between actual and expected demand.

Priority should be given to products that:

* have a material forecast variance;
* are below the target inventory coverage range;
* are classified as critical inventory;
* show potential overstock; or
* have significant incoming supply scheduled.

A forecast review may result in the forecast being maintained, increased, or decreased.

## 11. Planning Principle

Forecasts represent expected future demand and are inherently uncertain.

The approved forecast should provide the standard demand baseline, while actual sales provide evidence about whether that expectation remains reasonable.

Supply planners should therefore use both signals appropriately:

**Forecast tells NovaTech what it expects to sell.**

**Actual sales tell NovaTech what it has actually sold.**

A difference between the two should trigger investigation when material, but should not automatically trigger a supply or forecast change.

## 12. Policy Summary

NovaTech uses the **approved weekly forecast as the primary forward-looking demand signal** for supply planning.

Historical sales are used to evaluate forecast performance and identify potential changes in demand.

A variance greater than **20% between the most recent four-week average actual sales and the corresponding average forecast** is considered material and should trigger forecast review.

Historical sales do not automatically replace the approved forecast, and isolated demand spikes should not automatically result in forecast or replenishment changes.
