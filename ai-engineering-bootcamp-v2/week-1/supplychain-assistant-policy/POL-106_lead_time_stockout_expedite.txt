## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how NovaTech Retail uses supplier lead time, projected stockout timing, and incoming-supply arrival dates to identify supply risk and determine when expedited supply should be considered.

The objective is to identify situations where inventory may be exhausted before replacement supply can become available while avoiding unnecessary expedite actions and associated costs.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all active products managed through NovaTech Retail's standard replenishment process.

For the current planning model, inventory and demand from NovaTech's online and physical retail channels are evaluated as a single combined planning position for each SKU.

## 3. Supplier Lead Time

Supplier lead time represents the expected time between placing a valid purchase order and the ordered supply becoming available to NovaTech.

Lead time is measured in weeks for standard supply-planning purposes.

Lead times may differ by SKU and supplier and must therefore be obtained from NovaTech's current approved product and supplier data.

This policy defines how lead time should be interpreted; it does not define a single standard lead time for every product.

## 4. Lead Time and Replenishment Planning

Supplier lead time must be considered when evaluating whether additional replenishment can arrive before existing inventory is exhausted.

For example:

* Available inventory: 600 units
* Approved weekly forecast: 150 units
* Current WOS: 4 weeks
* Supplier lead time: 6 weeks
* No confirmed incoming supply

Although the product is currently within NovaTech's standard 4–6 WOS target range, a new standard order placed today would not be expected to arrive for six weeks.

The current inventory position should therefore not be interpreted without considering when replacement supply can realistically become available.

## 5. Projected Stockout

A projected stockout occurs when available and confirmed incoming inventory is expected to be exhausted before sufficient replacement supply becomes available.

For a basic assessment with constant weekly demand:

**Estimated Weeks to Stockout = Available Inventory / Approved Weekly Demand**

Where confirmed incoming supply exists, its quantity and arrival timing must also be considered.

A projected stockout is a forward-looking risk indicator and does not mean that the product is already out of stock.

## 6. Stockout Risk Before New Standard Supply

A SKU is considered exposed to potential stockout risk when expected inventory is insufficient to support demand until the earliest feasible arrival of new standard replenishment.

For example:

* Available inventory: 300 units
* Approved weekly forecast: 100 units
* Current coverage: 3 weeks
* Supplier lead time: 6 weeks
* No confirmed incoming supply

Inventory is expected to be exhausted approximately three weeks before a new standard order could arrive.

This represents a potential stockout exposure requiring replenishment review.

## 7. Stockout Risk with Confirmed Incoming Supply

Confirmed incoming supply must be evaluated according to its expected arrival date.

For example:

* Available inventory: 200 units
* Approved weekly forecast: 100 units
* Current coverage: 2 weeks
* Confirmed incoming supply: 600 units
* Expected arrival: 5 weeks

Current inventory is expected to be exhausted in approximately two weeks, while the confirmed supply is expected in five weeks.

This creates an estimated three-week stockout exposure despite the existence of a confirmed purchase order.

The treatment of confirmed incoming supply is defined in POL-104.

## 8. Expedite Definition

An expedite is an action intended to bring confirmed or required supply into NovaTech earlier than the standard or currently expected arrival date.

An expedite may involve:

* requesting an earlier supplier ship date;
* prioritizing production or order processing;
* using a faster approved transportation method; or
* accelerating an existing purchase order where the supplier supports the change.

Expedited supply may result in additional cost and should therefore be used only when justified by a material supply risk.

## 9. When Expedite Should Be Considered

An expedite should be considered when a projected stockout is expected to occur before the next confirmed or feasible standard supply arrival and the resulting inventory gap represents a material business risk.

Factors supporting an expedite may include:

* projected inventory reaching zero before confirmed supply arrives;
* confirmed incoming supply being delayed beyond the expected stockout date;
* sustained demand above forecast materially accelerating inventory consumption;
* an approved forecast increase creating an earlier stockout; or
* standard replenishment lead time being insufficient to prevent the projected stockout.

Critical inventory status under POL-101 should increase the priority of the review but does not automatically require an expedite.

## 10. When Expedite Is Not Required

An expedite should not normally be recommended when confirmed incoming supply is expected to arrive before available inventory is exhausted.

For example:

* Available inventory: 300 units
* Approved weekly forecast: 100 units
* Current coverage: 3 weeks
* Confirmed incoming supply: 500 units
* Expected arrival: 2 weeks

Although current WOS is below NovaTech's target range, the confirmed supply is expected to arrive before the projected stockout.

Based on these conditions alone, an expedite would not be required.

## 11. Expedite Quantity

Where an expedite is justified, the expedited quantity should be sufficient to address the expected supply gap without unnecessarily creating excess inventory.

The entire outstanding purchase-order quantity does not automatically need to be expedited.

For example, where 1,000 units are scheduled to arrive but only 200 units are required to bridge the projected inventory gap, planners should evaluate whether a partial expedite is operationally possible.

Any quantity adjustment must comply with applicable supplier constraints defined in POL-105.

## 12. Delayed Purchase Orders

A delay to confirmed incoming supply must trigger reassessment when the revised arrival date materially changes the projected inventory position.

For example:

**Original position**

* Estimated stockout: Week 5
* Confirmed PO arrival: Week 4

No inventory gap is expected.

**After supplier delay**

* Estimated stockout: Week 5
* Revised PO arrival: Week 7

The revised position creates an estimated two-week inventory gap.

An expedite or alternative replenishment action should therefore be considered.

## 13. Demand Changes and Stockout Risk

Stockout timing must be reassessed when expected demand changes materially.

Higher-than-forecast demand may cause inventory to be exhausted earlier than previously expected.

Lower-than-forecast demand may extend available coverage and reduce or eliminate a previously identified stockout risk.

Demand and forecast changes should be interpreted according to POL-102.

## 14. Expedite Recommendation

An expedite recommendation should identify:

* the affected SKU;
* the projected stockout timing;
* the next expected supply arrival;
* the estimated inventory gap;
* the quantity potentially required to cover the gap; and
* the reason standard replenishment is insufficient.

Where the available information is insufficient to determine stockout timing or incoming-supply timing, an expedite should not be recommended without further review.

## 15. Planning Principle

Low inventory and stockout risk are related but are not the same condition.

A product may have low current WOS but no projected stockout because confirmed supply will arrive in time.

Conversely, a product may currently have healthy WOS but still face future stockout risk when supplier lead time is longer than the remaining inventory coverage.

Expedite decisions must therefore compare **when inventory is expected to run out** with **when replacement supply is expected to become available**.

## 16. Policy Summary

Supplier lead time represents the expected time required for new replenishment to become available to NovaTech and may differ by SKU and supplier.

A projected stockout occurs when inventory is expected to be exhausted before sufficient replacement supply becomes available.

An expedite should be considered when the projected stockout occurs before the next confirmed or feasible standard supply arrival and the resulting gap represents a material business risk.

Critical or below-target WOS does not automatically require an expedite.

The core expedite decision is based on the timing relationship between **projected stockout** and **expected supply arrival**.
