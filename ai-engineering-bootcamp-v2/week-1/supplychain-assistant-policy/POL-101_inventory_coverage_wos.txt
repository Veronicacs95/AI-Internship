## 1. Purpose

This policy defines NovaTech Retail's inventory coverage standards and the use of Weeks of Supply (WOS) for assessing inventory health and supporting replenishment planning.

The purpose of these standards is to provide a consistent method for identifying products that are below target coverage, critically low, within a healthy inventory range, above target, or potentially overstocked.

This policy also defines the standard planning target used when calculating replenishment requirements.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all active products managed through NovaTech Retail's standard replenishment process.

For the current planning model, inventory and demand are evaluated as a single combined inventory pool across NovaTech's retail and online channels.

Unless otherwise specified by another approved policy, WOS is the primary inventory coverage indicator used for routine supply planning.

## 3. Weeks of Supply

Weeks of Supply represents the number of weeks that available inventory is expected to support demand at the current planning rate.

For standard current-coverage assessment:

**WOS = Available Inventory / Average Weekly Demand**

For example, if a product has 600 units of available inventory and average weekly demand of 150 units:

**WOS = 600 / 150 = 4 weeks**

The product therefore has four weeks of current inventory coverage.

WOS is a coverage indicator. It does not, by itself, determine whether a purchase order should be created, increased, reduced, delayed, or expedited.

## 4. Standard Inventory Coverage

NovaTech's **healthy inventory range is 4 to 6 weeks of supply**.

Within this range, NovaTech uses a **standard planning target of 5 WOS** for replenishment calculations.

The distinction is:

* **4–6 WOS:** healthy operating range;
* **5 WOS:** standard calculation target used when determining the desired inventory position.

The standard inventory coverage bands are:

* **Below 2 WOS:** Critical
* **2 to below 4 WOS:** Below target
* **4 to 6 WOS:** Healthy range
* **Above 6 to 8 WOS:** Above target
* **Above 8 WOS:** Potential overstock

These thresholds are planning indicators and do not, by themselves, authorize a purchase-order change.

## 5. Standard Planning Target

For standard replenishment calculations, NovaTech uses a **5-WOS planning target**.

The target inventory quantity may be estimated as:

**Target Inventory = Approved Average Weekly Demand × 5**

For example:

* Approved average weekly demand: 100 units
* Standard planning target: 5 WOS

**Target Inventory = 100 × 5 = 500 units**

The 5-WOS target provides a single deterministic planning point within NovaTech's healthy 4–6 WOS range.

The planning target should be used to calculate an inventory requirement, but the resulting requirement must still be evaluated against incoming supply, lead time, supplier constraints, and expected future demand before an order recommendation is finalized.

## 6. Below-Target Inventory

A product with less than **4 WOS** is considered below the healthy inventory range and should be reviewed.

A below-target position indicates that current inventory coverage may be insufficient to maintain NovaTech's desired stock position.

The difference between current inventory and the 5-WOS planning target may be used as an initial replenishment requirement.

For example:

* Available inventory: 300 units
* Approved average weekly demand: 100 units
* Current WOS: 3
* Standard planning target: 5 WOS
* Target inventory: 500 units

The initial gap to target is:

**500 - 300 = 200 units**

This 200-unit gap represents an initial planning requirement, not an automatic purchase-order quantity.

Before changing the replenishment plan, the planner must consider confirmed incoming supply, open purchase orders, supplier lead time, and applicable ordering constraints.

## 7. Critical Inventory

A product with less than **2 WOS** is classified as critical inventory.

Critical products should receive priority planning review because available inventory may be consumed before replacement supply can become available.

Critical status identifies elevated supply risk but does not automatically require an expedited order.

The planner must evaluate:

* expected demand before the next supply arrival;
* confirmed incoming supply;
* supplier lead time; and
* projected stockout timing.

Expedite rules are defined in POL-106.

## 8. Above-Target Inventory

A product with more than **6 WOS** is considered above the healthy inventory range.

A position above 6 WOS and up to 8 WOS does not automatically represent excess inventory, but planners should monitor expected demand and incoming supply.

Additional replenishment should normally be reviewed carefully when projected coverage is already above the healthy range.

## 9. Potential Overstock

A product with more than **8 WOS** is classified as potential overstock.

Potential overstock should trigger a review of expected demand and future incoming supply.

Where appropriate, planners should evaluate whether future replenishment can be reduced or delayed.

Any change to an existing purchase order must follow the applicable replenishment and purchase-order policies.

## 10. Current WOS and Projected WOS

Current WOS and projected WOS represent different planning views.

**Current WOS** describes the inventory coverage available today.

**Projected WOS** describes the expected inventory coverage at a future planning point after expected demand and relevant incoming supply have been considered.

For example, a SKU may have 4 WOS today but have a supplier lead time of 6 weeks.

If no incoming supply exists, the SKU may stock out before a newly placed standard order can arrive despite currently being within the healthy range.

Similarly, a SKU may have only 2 WOS today but have sufficient confirmed supply arriving next week to restore inventory to the healthy range.

Current WOS must therefore not be used as the only measure of future supply risk.

## 11. Use of WOS in Replenishment Decisions

WOS must not be used in isolation to make replenishment decisions.

The 5-WOS planning target provides a standard basis for calculating the desired inventory quantity, but the final replenishment requirement depends on the projected future inventory position.

Supply-planning decisions should therefore consider, where relevant:

* current available inventory;
* approved weekly demand forecast;
* recent actual sales;
* confirmed incoming supply;
* open purchase orders;
* expected supply-arrival dates;
* supplier lead time;
* MOQ and order multiples; and
* projected inventory at the relevant future planning point.

The treatment of these factors is defined in the related NovaTech planning policies.

## 12. Replenishment Requirement

For a basic current-position assessment:

**Initial Replenishment Requirement = Target Inventory - Available Inventory**

where:

**Target Inventory = Average Weekly Demand × 5 WOS**

If the result is zero or negative, no additional quantity is required to reach the standard planning target based on current inventory alone.

If the result is positive, the quantity represents an initial planning gap.

This initial gap must not automatically become the final order quantity.

The calculation must subsequently account for:

* confirmed incoming supply;
* demand expected before supply arrival;
* supplier lead time; and
* supplier ordering constraints.

The final recommendation may therefore differ from the initial gap-to-target calculation.

## 13. Planning Principle

NovaTech distinguishes between an **inventory health range** and a **planning calculation target**.

The healthy range of **4–6 WOS** indicates whether inventory coverage is broadly within the desired operating position.

The **5-WOS planning target** provides a single consistent point for calculating inventory requirements.

This distinction prevents ambiguity when converting a WOS policy into units.

For example, a planner should not have to choose arbitrarily between ordering enough inventory for 4 weeks or 6 weeks. The standard calculation uses 5 WOS unless another approved policy or exception applies.

The result must then be evaluated against future supply and demand conditions before action is taken.

## 14. Policy Summary

NovaTech uses WOS as its standard inventory coverage indicator.

The **healthy inventory range is 4–6 WOS**, while **5 WOS is the standard planning target used for replenishment calculations**.

Inventory below **4 WOS** requires review, inventory below **2 WOS** is critical, and inventory above **8 WOS** is potential overstock.

For standard calculations:

**Target Inventory = Approved Average Weekly Demand × 5**

The resulting gap to target represents an initial planning requirement only.

WOS and the 5-WOS target must not be used in isolation to make purchase-order decisions.

Final replenishment decisions must consider projected demand, confirmed incoming supply, arrival timing, supplier lead time, and supplier ordering constraints.
